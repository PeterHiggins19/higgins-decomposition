## The answer: yes, and it shows "the other remains other"

Reading each term's frequency in the public Google Books English corpus as an attention proxy, closing the four
into a composition per year, and reading it with Hˢ:

| part | mean share (1900–2019) | role |
|---|---|---|
| **science** | **58.6%** | the dominant known |
| **belief** | **30.5%** | the other method, of the same order |
| **physics** | **10.8%** | the measured sub-field |
| **supersymmetry** | **0.1%** (peak 0.6% in 1985) | **the other that remains other** |

Two readings, and their contrast is the whole point:

- **In share, the other remains other.** Science and belief together hold ~89% — *both sides give the whole*,
  and they sit at the same order of magnitude (the science:belief log-ratio drifts only 0.49 → 0.56 across the
  century; science edges ahead, but the two methods coexist). Physics is the measured tenth. Supersymmetry is
  **vanishing** — a mean share of one part in a thousand, never more than 0.6% even at its 1985 peak. It
  completes the four, and it stays negligible.
- **In motion, the other is the helmsman.** Yet in **log-ratio velocity**, supersymmetry is the **biggest
  mover by far** — clr-velocity energy **29.9 vs ~10** for each of the others. It was essentially absent before
  ~1970, erupted into the corpus (proposed in physics in the early 1970s), peaked in 1985, and has receded
  since. So **the smallest part is the largest mover**: it carries the *surprise* — the information — while
  never carrying the *mass.*

That is exactly the figure Peter drew. Supersymmetry, like its physics, **completes the picture and remains
other**: it would close the symmetry, it moves the most when it appears, and it never becomes substance — in
the corpus as in the collider, the superpartner is the part that is always almost-there and never confirmed.
The effective dimension of the mean composition is **2.5** — the four-part whole really lives in about two and
a half parts: the two methods, a measured field, and a ghost.

## Why this honestly answers the question (and where it stops)

Peter asked whether such a composition could *even* be measured. It can — **as a proxy** — and the proxy is
honest about being one. What is measured is **textual attention** (how often each word appears in books), read
relationally. The relational read is what makes it interesting: the share view and the motion view disagree,
and the disagreement *is* the "known vs other" structure. But the fences are firm and load-bearing:

- It measures **words, not the things** — not the truth of science or belief, not the existence of
  supersymmetry, not the "amount" of any of them in the world.
- The four parts are **not disjoint** (physics ⊂ science; supersymmetry ⊂ physics), so this is a composition
  of four chosen *terms'* relative attention, **not** an ontological partition of reality.
- **Supersymmetry remains experimentally unconfirmed** (no superpartners observed as of 2025); the corpus
  counts only the word's rise and fall.
- *"Science has religious origins"* is real historical context (natural philosophy grew partly inside
  religious institutions), but it is **not** what this measures — it frames the question, it is not a result
  here.
- And the meaning of the figure — whether "the other remains other" says something true about science and
  belief, or about the cosmos — is the **operator's leap (Breaker 16)**. The instrument measured the attention
  and its shape; what that shape *means* is yours.
