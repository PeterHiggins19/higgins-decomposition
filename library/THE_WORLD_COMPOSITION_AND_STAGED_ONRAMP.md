# The world read as a composition — who is near the front line, who is far, and how to speak to each

*Author: Peter Higgins (human authorship for all claims); AI-assisted per HUF-STD-001. 2026-06-26. A test of
the waters: turn the instrument on the **world itself** and read it as a composition — which fields do what,
where their data lives, who is **closest** to the compositional front line and who is **farthest**, and then
stage the on-ramp so each group meets **exactly the depth of guidance its distance calls for.** The targeting
is real — we anticipate the audience to keep the message coherent — but it is **never displayed**, because a
system that visibly anticipates a person makes them nervous. Map + receipt: `world_composition_map.py`
(`58ee852a537700f4`). Honest-broker tiered; Peter is the sole gate; nothing posted.*

## The read (one finding)

The world divides not into "uses Hˢ / doesn't," but along **two axes that the instrument itself makes
visible:** how compositional a field's data actually is (*need*), and how much that field **already reads it
that way** (*literacy*). Multiply them two ways and the whole audience sorts itself:

- **front-line proximity = need × literacy** — high where a field already operates in ratios. These are
  **peers,** not converts.
- **guidance depth = need × (1 − literacy)** — high where the data is compositional but the field reads
  **totals.** This is the **blindness gap:** the people who need it most and see it least, and therefore the
  ones who need the most scaffolding — and for whom the reveal is worth the most.
- **the door = need alone** — below a threshold a field simply **isn't compositional,** and the honest move
  is the friendly redirect, not a pitch.

So the distance from the front line is not a judgment of a field's worth — it is just *how far its current
reading is from the relational one,* and that distance is exactly the right setting for the guidance dial.

| **Near / mid** | energy mix (EMBER), demography/census, marketing share | **Stage 2–3.** One re-computable win on *their own* data; name the helmsman (the part doing the moving); hand them the gauge. A light bridge, not a lecture. |

The sharpest single result the read returns: **clinical labs sit farthest from the front line and carry the
deepest guidance need** (proximity 0.21, depth 0.61) — blood panels are profoundly compositional yet read as
absolute levels. That is the largest blindness gap in the set, and the clearest illustration of why the far
band is the high-value band, not the low one. *(Fenced: research/QA framing only — never clinical or

## The staged on-ramp (guidance scaled to distance)

The principle is one line: **the farther a field is from the front line, the more the on-ramp carries it —
and the more it speaks in the field's own everyday terms rather than the system's.** A peer gets the
instrument and a receipt. A mid-field gets one win on their own data. A far field gets the full magic show,
built entirely out of things they already own, one step at a time, each step paying out a read they couldn't
get before. The depth is the only thing that changes; the honesty rails (a real win each contact, the
friendly door, let-them-recompute, the human keeps the gate) are identical at every band.

This is the contact-length doctrine, now *addressed*: the same graduated reveal, pointed at each band by the
map, so no one is met above or below where they live. Pitching the full spectrum to a peer wastes their time;
pitching peer-level shorthand to a far field buys glaze. The map is what lets the reveal land at the right
depth every time.

## The non-obvious rule (anticipate, but never display the anticipation)

Here is the part that needs care. We **do** anticipate the audience — that is what makes the communication
coherent instead of generic. But the anticipation must stay **invisible**, because a system that visibly
predicts a person reads as surveillance, and people get nervous when a tool seems to know them. The
resolution is a single discipline:

> **Tailor the depth; never show the dial. Speak each field in its own data, not in the meta-model of the
> listener.**

Concretely: a far-band reader meets *bread, a gas tank, their own blood panel* — never the words "you are in
the blindness gap." A peer meets *a receipt and a second viewpoint* — never "you are a front-line peer." The
relevance is felt as **care and fluency in their domain;** the map that produced it stays **back-stage**, the
same place the workshop litter lives. Done right, the reader's experience is "this speaks my language and
respects my time," not "this has profiled me." That is the difference between meeting someone where they live
and showing them you measured them.

And the ethical fence is firm: this is *meeting-where-they-are,* the way a good teacher pitches to a student's
level without announcing the assessment — **not deception.** No fact is bent to the audience; only the depth
and the entry-prop are chosen. The claim each band hears is the same true, re-computable claim; what differs
is how far we walk in with them. We reveal and invite; we never manipulate; the human keeps the gate.

## Honest scope

- **T1 (measured/deterministic):** the *math* of the sort — proximity, depth, door-fit, the banding — is
  deterministic and receipted (`58ee852a537700f4`, reproduces). The relational facts each band is shown
  (silent drift, the helmsman, common-mode rejection) are themselves measured elsewhere in the repo.
- **T2 (designed judgment):** the per-field axis scores (closure / proportion-action / multipart / literacy)
  are **reasoned estimates, not a survey.** The banding is only as good as those inputs. The staging and the
  non-obvious rule are doctrine — held intent, to be refined by real contact.
- **T3 (to be earned):** that this staging actually raises comprehension per contact. Falsifier: survey real
  practitioners in each field for need and literacy, refit the map, and measure whether depth-matched on-ramps
  outperform a flat one.
- **Not claimed:** that any field's people are uniform (a field is not a person), or that the map targets
  individuals — it sorts **fields by how their data behaves.** **Nothing is posted; this plans the address,
  it does not send it. Peter is the sole gate.**
