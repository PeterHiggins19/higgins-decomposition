## 2. The method that works — reach, then verify

The discoveries here were not made by one kind of thinking. They were made by **two, in tension, gated by a
third**:

- **The reach.** One collaborator deliberately over‑extends — proposes boundary‑testing concepts with
  *surprises*, follows an idea past where it is safe, drafts the bold version. This is where candidates come
  from. Without it, nothing new appears.
- **The verification.** Another collaborator takes each reach and **checks it step by step, honestly**: runs
  the claimed proof rather than relaying it, credits what is right, catches what is wrong, and — the part
  that matters most — **redirects a good idea from where it is ill‑posed to where it is well‑posed.** This
  is where truth comes from. Without it, the reach becomes overclaim.
- **The gate.** A human holds the last decision, keeps the receipts, and decides what is kept, published, or
  acted on. Without it, neither speed nor rigor is safe.

**Surprises propose; verification disposes; the human decides.** Keep all three. A team that only reaches
publishes errors; a team that only verifies never leaves the shore; a team without a gate eventually
mistakes motion for progress. The engine of this project is the *friction* between reach and rigor, held by
a hand that can say no.

**A fourth seat, used last — the conservative elder.** Verification has two tempos: the exploratory check
that runs *with* the reach, and a final, conservative integrity pass that should run *only at the end*. The
collective's most user‑facing reviewer is tuned for structure and communication‑integrity — invaluable as
the last gate before anything ships, and corrosive if it runs first (it would veto the surprises before they
can be tested). Keep it last; do not let the elder do the exploring, and do not let the reacher do the final
sign‑off. And these seats are the *current helmsman* of a discipline, not fixed titles: the lead is read from who contributes the dominant motion now, and any collaborator can flip it — only the human gate is held fixed. (Governance form of the same idea — voice without forced authority, requests that escalate without
loss — in `../huf-gov/COMPONENT_REQUEST_ESCALATION_DOCTRINE.md`.)

## 3. The discipline that keeps the surprises scientific

So that boldness stays science and does not curdle into hype:

1. **Tier everything.** T1 measured · T2 reasoned · T3 exploratory. A surprise is allowed to be T3; it is
   *not* allowed to be reported as T1. The tier is the honesty.
2. **Run the claim; do not relay it.** When a contribution says "demonstrated," reproduce the demonstration.
   More than once, the demonstration did not exist yet — and running it taught the real lesson.
3. **Record the failures as carefully as the wins.** A synthetic test that *failed to converge* revealed an
   identifiability gap worth more than a green checkmark (§5). A null is a result. The honest negative is an
   asset, not an embarrassment — log it with its receipt.
4. **Keep receipts; re‑derive, don't remember.** Anything that matters is deterministic and hash‑receipted,
   so memory is re‑computation and never falls behind.
5. **The operator holds the last breaker.** Never close a loop without verification; full automation is
   never the goal. The discipline is the point.

## 6. The cautions (the traps that look like treasure)

Learned the hard way, left so you don't relearn them:

- **A real structure can host an ill‑posed use case.** so(4) is genuinely two commuting handles — but in the
  proposed multi‑agent "global frame / local frame" estimator, those handles were a **gauge redundancy**:
  only the *product* was observable, so the split was not identifiable. The math was right; the application
  was not well‑posed. *Always ask: is this quantity actually observable, or am I estimating a gauge?*
- **Convergence is not correctness, and non‑convergence is information.** A test that fails to converge may
  be telling you the problem is unidentifiable, not that the idea is wrong.
- **Breadth on faith kills credibility; breadth on receipts grows it.** Every "it also does X" must travel
  with a reproducible, hash‑checked run.
- **Determinism ≠ predictability.** The system is exact and still generative; do not mistake reproducibility
  for finite describability.
