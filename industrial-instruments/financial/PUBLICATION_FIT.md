## The space — four neighbours, none of them us

1. **Static compositional finance (statistics).** The active, nearest community. Allocations and ratios
   read *compositionally but as a snapshot*: "New financial ratios based on the compositional data
   methodology" (arXiv:2210.11138); the CoDaWork 2026 contributions of **Vega Baquero & Santolino**
   (allocation *proportionality* indices) and **Keivani & Coenders** (compositional log-ratios to
   *predict bankruptcy*). These characterise and classify. They do not read motion.

2. **Stochastic Portfolio Theory (Fernholz, 2002).** The deepest and most important neighbour. SPT is
   *descriptive* (not normative) and studies **market weights** — each name's share of total
   capitalisation — as a **process on the simplex** over time, deriving functionally generated portfolios
   and relative-arbitrage results. It already lives on the compositional simplex in motion. **But it is a
   stochastic, model-theoretic framework** (continuous semimartingales, probability measures, drift/excess
   growth). This is the citation and the contrast that a reviewer will most want to see addressed.

3. **Information geometry of compositional/financial data.** Kaniadakis's information geometry of
   compositional data, with financial-risk applications — velocity/curvature on the simplex under a
   *Fisher-Rao-family* metric (vs our Aitchison metric). Theoretical; not a deterministic instrument.

4. **Econophysics "market geometry."** Statistical/geometric distributional models of indices (e.g.
   "price runs" and geometric distributions in DJIA/NASDAQ/Nikkei). Reads price series statistically, not
   the allocation composition deterministically.

## Where Hˢ fits — the gap, stated precisely

Hˢ is a **deterministic, non-stochastic, hash-receipted descriptive instrument** that reads an *observed*
compositional financial trajectory as **kinematics** — arrow of intent, helmsman, character
(diffusive↔directed), effective dimensionality, dated regime changes, and each part's position. It is
**not** a model (unlike SPT), **not** an optimiser, **not** a classifier (unlike the static CoDa stats),
**not** a forecast, and **not** advice. It complements all four neighbours and competes with none:

| neighbour | what it does | what Hˢ adds |
|---|---|---|
| Static CoDa-finance | proportionality / prediction, snapshot | the *motion*: heading + dated reorganisations |
| Stochastic Portfolio Theory | stochastic **model** of the weight process | a **deterministic, model-free, reproducible read** of the realised path (no semimartingale, no probability) |
| Information geometry | Fisher-Rao theory of velocity/curvature | an **Aitchison-metric instrument** on real data, with a receipt |
| Econophysics market geometry | statistical price-series distributions | the **allocation composition** read as a navigable vector map |

The distinctive triple (per the repo's `papers/PRIOR_ART_compositional_kinematics_2026-06-14.md`): a
**descriptive instrument over observed trajectories**, in the **Aitchison metric**, with **mass-weighted
momentum (arrow of intent ≠ helmsman)** and a **noise-bounded honesty tower** — now demonstrated on a real
financial composition, reproducible to a hash.

## Why a field of many viewpoints is the opening

Bulls, bears, quants, allocators, regulators — all argue meaning. None of them disputes *what the
allocation did*, once it is read deterministically with a receipt anyone can reproduce. Hˢ takes no side;
it hands every viewpoint the same map and lets them argue from a shared, audit-able fact. That neutrality
is the attention hook and the honest-broker posture at once.

## How to modify the approach (so it fits and earns attention)

1. **Lead with the SPT contrast.** Open by placing Hˢ next to Stochastic Portfolio Theory: both descriptive,
   both on the simplex in motion — but SPT is *stochastic-model* and *prescriptive of strategies*, Hˢ is
   *deterministic, model-free, and prescriptive of nothing*. This is the sharpest, most defensible framing
   for a finance reviewer.
2. **Add a null / robustness pass.** Run the repo's time-shuffle / deceptive-drift null (as in P2) on the
   sector series to show the **regime changes survive a null** and are not artifacts of autocorrelation.
   This is the single most credibility-bearing addition for a quantitative audience.
3. **Hook to sector rotation / the business cycle.** Frame the worked reading against the practitioner's
   existing mental model (sector rotation), then show the instrument *dates the rotation's turns* from the
   composition alone — familiar question, new exactness.
4. **Headline reproducibility.** For a financial-data audience, the receipt + open code is the differentiator
   from every black-box signal vendor: *run it, get the same hash.* Make that the abstract's spine.
5. **Hold the honest envelope visibly.** Descriptive, not predictive; not advice; Tier-1 reading / Tier-3
   meaning; holds-or-warns. In finance this *increases* credibility — an instrument that refuses to bluff.
6. **Keep neutrality explicit.** A shared instrument for a field of many viewpoints; takes no market view.
