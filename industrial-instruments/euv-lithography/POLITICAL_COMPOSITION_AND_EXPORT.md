## 1. Why this study exists

The EUV/advanced‑node case is powerful, and that is precisely why it touches more than engineering. Leading‑edge
semiconductor manufacturing is one of the most **export‑controlled and geopolitically contested** technology
domains in the world. A method that helps advanced‑node *yield* — even a thin, read‑only analytical layer — sits
near that sensitivity. The responsible move is to **map the political composition, separate what is publishable
from what is not, and defer the sensitive decision upward** to the governance system we are part of.

## 2. The political composition of the field (cited, factual)

Read the world of advanced lithography as a composition of roles — a simplex of who holds what:

- **The tool monopoly.** **ASML (Netherlands)** holds a global monopoly on EUV lithography; EUV is listed under
  the **Wassenaar Arrangement** as a **dual‑use technology requiring export licenses**.³ ⁴
- **The allied control bloc.** The **US, Netherlands, and Japan** coordinate controls on advanced lithography and
  equipment. EUV machines **have never been sold to China**; the US persuaded the Netherlands not to renew
  EUV‑to‑China licenses (2019); the Netherlands **expanded** licensing of ASML equipment in 2025; proposed US
  measures (e.g. the MATCH Act) would extend even to **DUV**.³ ⁴
- **The restricted destination.** **China** — ~20% of ASML 2026 sales (down from ~33% in 2025) as controls
  tighten; pursuing indigenous lithography ("the lithography loophole").³ ⁴ On **2026‑06‑19** ASML reiterated it
  had delivered neither a complete EUV system nor EUV‑specific components to China.³
- **The packaging/test & photonics tier.** A broader, **less front‑end‑sensitive** ring — advanced packaging,
  test, and silicon photonics — where **Canada** actually participates (IBM Bromont / C2MI; see
  `INDUSTRY_IMPACT_AND_OFFERING.md` §6).¹ ²

*This is a factual landscape, not a measured Hˢ run.* We are **not** asserting political metrics or running the
engine on geopolitical data — that would manufacture authority we do not have. The compositional framing is a
*lens* for governance reasoning, tiered T3.

## 3. The export question for *this* work — principle vs how‑to

The decisive distinction, and the heart of Peter's instruction:

| layer | what it is | disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **The principle** | the abstract compositional method (closure → log‑ratio → kinematic read → coherence gate → receipt); the mathematics; the published P‑series | **Publishable.** Basic science, already public in abstract. No controlled content. |
| **The applied how‑to** | a specific, tuned recipe for advancing **advanced‑node yield/process** on real leading‑edge tools | **Withhold.** Even a read‑only analytics layer, if it materially advances leading‑edge manufacturing, can fall within the spirit (and possibly the letter) of dual‑use / "emerging & foundational technology" controls. **Defer the release decision upward.** |

The rule we adopt: **publish the principle; gate the applied how‑to; let a higher governance authority decide on
the applied release.** This is conservative on purpose. It does not assume the analytics layer *is* controlled —
it assumes we are **not the right party to make that call alone**, and we route it to those who are.

## 4. "Another governance system we are a composition of"

HUF is itself a **sub‑composition** of larger governance systems — a national jurisdiction (Canada), and through
it an allied order (Wassenaar and partner controls). The same principle the instrument lives by — *the part
reports up; control flows down only when the larger system authorizes it; the operator at the top holds the
breaker* — applies to **us**. So:

- **The principle** flows out (published), because basic science is the part's contribution to the whole.
- **The applied how‑to** does **not** flow out on our authority; it is **offered up** to the governance system we
  are part of, which holds the equivalent of Breaker 16 for sensitive technology release.
- **Canada‑specific:** Canada is a **Wassenaar participating state** with its own **Export Control List** and
  allied‑coordination obligations; any sensitive transfer is properly routed through that national authority.
  *(Procedural fact; not legal advice — competent export‑control counsel decides specifics. T3.)*

This is not timidity; it is the instrument's own doctrine applied honestly to itself. We built a governance layer
that keeps the human at the top and control gated — it would be incoherent to exempt ourselves from it on the one
case where the stakes are highest.
