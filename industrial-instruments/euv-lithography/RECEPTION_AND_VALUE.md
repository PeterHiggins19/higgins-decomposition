## 1. How would it be received? (the honest spread)

Unsolicited offers to government usually move slowly, and the honest base rate is "politely filed." So the
realistic default is the one you already named: *a head start, if they even read it.* That said, this offering
has several features that push it above the default — and some real friction working against it.

**What helps it land**

- **Timely.** Semiconductor sovereignty is a live policy priority; the Nov 2025 Bromont/C2MI commitment shows
  active appetite and a desk that funds this exact area.¹
- **Real domestic fit.** It maps onto Canada's *actual* strength — advanced packaging, test, photonics — not a
  fantasy fab. The lower‑sensitivity on‑ramp is genuinely Canadian.
- **Low friction.** No money asked, no exclusivity, public‑good framing, science published regardless. An offer
  that *gives* is easier to accept than one that *sells*.
- **Credible tone.** Honest tiering and reproducible receipts read as engineering, not hype — which is rare in
  unsolicited tech offers and lowers the "too‑good‑to‑be‑true" reflex.

**What works against it**

- **No champion, no third‑party validation yet.** Government desks act on things a known party vouches for.
- **Unfamiliar method.** Compositional analysis is niche; "read the ratios" needs a *measured* pilot result to
  be credible to a process engineer.
- **Program cycles & "not invented here."** Even a good fit waits on intake windows and internal priorities.

**Most honest expectation:** low probability of fast uptake; a non‑trivial probability of a *serious read* if it
reaches the right desk **with one measured pilot result** attached. Because the cost of offering is ~zero and the
science is published either way, the expected value of making the offer is positive even at low odds — and the
world benefits regardless of whether Canada acts.

**What would most move the needle (in order):** (1) one real pilot result on public or partner packaging/test
data; (2) a credible academic/industry co‑sign (e.g., via the C2MI ecosystem); (3) a named program officer to
carry it; (4) routing to the right desk (ISED's semiconductor strategy, NRC‑IRAP, or an academic partner) rather
than a cold general inbox.

## 2. What is it worth, if actually applied? (Tier‑3 envelope)

The honest method is the constellation one: a **public base × an explicit assumed small fraction**, with every
fraction flagged as an assumption. Hˢ is a *thin analytical layer* — it improves reading and timing; it does not
re‑plumb the fab — so the value is bounded and per‑line/per‑ecosystem, **not** "transforms the industry."

*Public anchors:* a single yield point ≈ **millions/yr per fab**, and small process variation swings yield
1–3%;² the **semiconductor yield‑management market** is ~**$2.1B (2025)**;² advanced packaging ~**$40B**, OSAT
services ~**$52.6B (2025)**, North America ~22.8% share.³

*The envelope (`value_envelope.py`, receipt `98b215dc`; all $M/yr, all assumed):*

| scope | assumed lever | range ($M/yr) |
|---|---|---|
| **A — one advanced line (pilot)** | recover 0.1–0.3 of a yield point on one line | **0.5 – 3** |
| **B — Canadian packaging/test ecosystem** | 0.1–0.5% efficiency/yield uplift on an assumed ~$2B base | **2 – 10** |
| **C — global‑standard upside** *(flagged, NOT summed)* | a few % of the yield‑management market *if* it became a recognized licensable layer | 21 – 85 |
| **Realistic near‑term (A few lines + B)** | — | **~$2.5 – 19M/yr** |

**The crux, said plainly:** every one of those fractions is an **assumption**. The load‑bearing one — *does the
ratio‑read actually recover yield, and how much* — is **unmeasured**. It could be larger, smaller, or **zero**.
Only a real pilot turns this from a guess into a number. So the honest headline is: *plausibly single‑digit to
low‑tens of millions per year at Canadian‑ecosystem scale if the unproven yield fraction holds — and nothing
asserted until measured.*

**The value that isn't in the table.** For Canada the bigger prize is probably **non‑dollar**: a sovereignty and
learning‑rate asset, and an auditable monitoring *standard* in a contested strategic domain — a head start that
is hard to price and may matter more than the direct cash. And the published science has **diffuse, global**
value that is, by design, *not* an appropriable Canadian dollar — that is the point of giving it to the world.
