## The composition today (2024, real IMF COFER, allocated reserves)

| currency | share |
|---|---|
| **US dollar** | **58%** |
| Euro | 20% |
| Japanese yen | 6% |
| British pound | 5% |
| Chinese renminbi | 2% |
| Nontraditional (AUD, CAD, CHF, other) | 9% |

**Effective dimension ≈ 3.5** of six buckets — the reserve money is a near‑duopoly of USD + EUR with a real tail.
The dominant‑currency arrow is, unmistakably, the **US dollar**.

## Follow the money — what the trajectory says

The single most‑watched number in global finance, read as the *top share* of a composition (IMF COFER, cited):

| year | USD share of allocated reserves |
|---|---|
| 1995 | ~58% |
| **2001** | **72% (peak)** |
| 2014 | ~65% |
| 2022 | 58% |
| 2024 | **58% — lowest since ~1995** |

**The dollar's share fell from 72% (2001) to 58% (2024)** — and a falling top share *is*, exactly, the reserve
composition **diversifying**: central banks spread into a wide range of smaller currencies (Australian and
Canadian dollars and others). This is the cited concentration metric itself — no estimate of the non‑USD split
needed to state it.

## The flow regime — laminar, not turbulent

Unlike the GDP layer, the reserve composition flows **laminar**: it re‑mixes *slowly and steadily* over decades,
and is **basically unchanged 2022 → 2024** — even the 2022 sanctions on Russia did *not* trigger a shock
reallocation out of dollars. The money moves like a slow current, not a churning one.

## The cross‑finding (this is the one worth keeping)

Two real public datasets, two layers of the same world, read by the same instrument — and they move in
**opposite directions**:

> **The world's economic *weight* is concentrating** (GDP layer, `d03048c3`: effective dimension **7.3 → 6.2**,
> toward a US–China duopoly) — **while the world's reserve *money* is diversifying** (COFER layer, `e339945f`:
> USD top share **72% → 58%**, spreading into more currencies).

The economy narrows toward two giants; the money it is denominated in spreads out. A scalar headline ("the dollar
is still ~58%", "world GDP is up") sees neither motion. The compositional read sees both — *and that they point
opposite ways.* That is the composition‑of‑compositions Peter asked for: GDP and reserves are two faces of the
monetary system, and reading them together says something neither says alone.
