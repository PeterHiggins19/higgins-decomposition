## The honest twist the share hides

> The dollar's **share** fell 72% (2001) → 58% (2024). But the **dollars themselves** grew from **≈ $1.5 T → ≈ $7.2 T**
> — roughly **five‑fold** — because total reserves swelled from ~$2 T to ~$12.4 T. **A falling share is not a
> falling holding.** "De‑dollarization" in *share* coincided with a *quintupling* in *dollars held.* The share
> study, alone, conceals that — which is precisely why the magnitude belongs next to it.

This is the number I had been carefully stepping around. It is real, cited, and anyone could finish it — so here
it is finished.

## The economy layer in dollars (World Bank, current US$)

- The ten economies studied totalled **≈ $40.2 T (2009) → ≈ $71.1 T (2023)** — about **67% of the ~$105 T** world
  economy.
- Absolute GDP added 2009 → 2023: **China ≈ +$13.1 T**, **United States ≈ +$12.8 T** — *both* grew, hugely, in
  dollars. **China's share rose because it grew faster, not because the US shrank.** (Japan actually *fell* ≈ −$1.1 T
  in current‑US$ — the yen effect again.)

Same lesson as reserves: **share‑motion and dollar‑motion are different faces** of one composition. The relational
read (shares, arrow, concentration) tells you the *direction of the mix*; the magnitude tells you the *size of the
stake*. You need both, and avoiding either is its own kind of blindness.
