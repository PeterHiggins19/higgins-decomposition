## 1. The world field, read as a composition

Pointing the same instrument at the world (`world_composition.py`, receipt `87fbaaa3`; illustrative public 2025
shares, blended metric, T3): the **arrow points at Asia‑Pacific** (~53%, the manufacturing mass: China, Taiwan,
South Korea, Japan), with the **United States** leading on value/design (~22%) and **Europe** (~12.7%, including
the Netherlands/ASML EUV monopoly). The field's **effective dimension is ~3.3** — it is really three big blocs
and a tail. **Canada is ~0.2%** of global semiconductor revenue (~$1B of a ~$600B+ field) — deep in that tail by
mass.¹ ²

## 2. But mass is the wrong axis for Canada

By **volume** Canada is tiny and always will be — the mass is in Asia and won't move. By **specialty** Canada sits
in the *leading cluster*:

- **Compound semiconductors** — the CPFC is North America's only end‑to‑end pure‑play compound‑semiconductor
  facility.²
- **Photonics & quantum** — a recognized strength; fresh federal focus (NRC, May 2026) on photonic semiconductors
  and AI.³ ⁴
- **Advanced packaging & test** — IBM Bromont (largest OSAT in North America) + C2MI.⁵
- **R&D / design hub** — 500+ companies, world‑class specialized expertise; ~$4.6B GDP, ~17,000 jobs.²

And one telling gap: **Canada is the only G7 nation without a national semiconductor strategy.**³ A gap is also an
**opening** — there is room to define a position rather than inherit one.

> The compositional read inverts the usual scoreboard: by mass Canada barely registers; by *specialty and
> trust‑infrastructure* it is in the front cluster. The instrument reads the axis that actually favors Canada.

## 3. How a small player gains momentum — move on the standard, not the fab

The momentum model (`87fbaaa3`, deterministic but **illustrative**): Canada's *volume* share is fixed and tiny
(~0.002). But influence in a **standards‑led quality layer** is not bought by volume — it is earned by moving
**early**. Model "influence" as volume_share + a standard‑layer weight that follows an adoption S‑curve, and
compare an early mover (starts now) to a late mover (waits five years):

| | early adopter | late adopter |
|---|---|---|
| influence by year ~7 | ~0.30 (near the cap) | ~0.09 |
| 12‑year **compounding lead** (area between the curves) | — | **1.47** |

Same tiny volume share; the early mover **compounds standard‑layer influence sooner and holds a persistent
lead** — the area between the curves is the momentum. A head start on the **standard** (an auditable, deterministic
monitoring/quality layer), not on the **fab**, is exactly the move a small, high‑R&D, no‑strategy‑yet country can
make to punch above its weight.

## 4. The boost loop (early adoption → ongoing momentum)

```
   adopt the auditable standard early  →  reference deployments (Bromont/C2MI/CPFC, packaging/test/photonics)
        →  a verifiable track record (receipts, not claims)  →  ecosystem + talent gather around it
        →  Canada becomes a reference for the quality/monitoring layer  →  influence > volume share
        →  feeds the national strategy the G7 gap leaves open  →  compounding momentum
```

Each step is small, real, and reversible; none requires winning on manufacturing mass. The science is published
to the world regardless; the *applied head start* is what compounds for the early steward.

## 5. Image / projection (kept honest)

The same composition that locates Canada is **verifiable** — anyone can re‑compute it — which is the right basis
for projection: a position you can *show*, not just assert. The honest projection is not "Canada leads
semiconductors" (it does not, by mass) but "**Canada can lead the trust‑and‑quality layer of advanced
manufacturing** — auditable, deterministic, early — a role its specialties and its open‑data culture already
fit." Evenhanded: this is a *case defenders would make*, tiered T3; the government weighs it.
