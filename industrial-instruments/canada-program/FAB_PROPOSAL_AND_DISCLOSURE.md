# A Canadian fab, outside‑supported — and the disclosure question (INTERNAL · PLANNING)

*Author: Peter Higgins (human authorship for all claims); AI‑assisted per HUF‑STD‑001. 2026‑06‑24. Two honest
questions: does a Canadian‑built, outside‑supported fab "powered by Hˢ" make sense — and would publishing the
science erase its advantage so fast it isn't worth it? Descriptive, tiered, **not financial or strategic
advice**. Peter is the sole gate; nothing transmitted.*

---

## 1. The premise is already half‑real (and that's the honest scope)

The hypothetical "Canadian‑built fab, supported by outside" is **not** hypothetical at the specialty tier — it is
**happening now**. In **May 2026** the federal government announced spinning the **Canadian Photonics Fabrication
Centre (CPFC)** into a **commercial pure‑play compound‑semiconductor (III‑V) / photonics foundry**, with **$350 M
new capital** (atop $115 M committed), partnered with CDEV to bring in private investors.¹ ² That is *exactly*
"Canadian‑built, outside‑supported" — a **specialty** foundry, where Canada genuinely leads.

So the realistic version of the question is **not** a $20–40B leading‑edge EUV logic megafab (TSMC Arizona ≈ $40B
per fab, $165B program; US fabs cost ~2× Taiwan and take ~2× as long; and the front end is export‑controlled).³
Canada's fit — and Canada's actual policy direction — is the **specialty / compound‑semiconductor / photonics /
advanced‑packaging** fab. A proposal should aim *there*, not at a megafab Canada will not build.

## 2. Where a fab's advantage actually lives (the disclosure answer)

The worry — "would it become public so fast there's no advantage?" — conflates two very different assets:

- **The method** (the Hˢ principle) — published *by design*, non‑exclusive, diffuses everywhere.
- **The fab** — capital, tacit process know‑how, accumulated yield‑learning, talent, ecosystem, location,
  supply‑chain. **Publishing the method makes none of these public.**

Read a fab's competitive advantage as a composition (`fab_advantage.py`, receipt `e03a7465`):

| source of advantage | share | copy‑resistant? |
|---|---|---|
| tacit process know‑how / accumulated yield‑learning | 19.6% | yes |
| capital & physical capacity | 17.4% | yes |
| talent & local ecosystem | 17.4% | yes |
| early operational‑learning lead (Hˢ‑accelerated, tacit) | 15.2% | yes |
| location / sovereignty / policy access | 13.0% | yes |
| supply‑chain relationships | 13.0% | yes |
| **the published method (Hˢ principle)** | **4.3%** | **no** |

**~96% of the advantage is copy‑resistant** — physical and tacit, untouched by publishing. **Publishing the method
erodes only ~4%.** So the honest answer is: **no, it does not become advantage‑free.** The advantage that survives
disclosure is precisely **the part you cannot write down** — the fab, the talent, and above all the *accumulated
operational learning*.

## 3. The correct role for Hˢ (don't over‑weight it)

"Powered by Hˢ" is the wrong frame for the *moat*. Hˢ is a **thin quality / yield‑learning accelerant**, not the
fab's core advantage. Its real contribution is the **15%** slice — it *accelerates the private, tacit operational
learning* — not the **4%** published‑method slice. In other words:

> Hˢ's value to a fab is **not a secret method; it is faster private learning.** The method is given to the world;
> the *learning it accelerates inside the fab stays inside the fab* — and compounds. That is the head‑start the
> momentum model already showed (`87fbaaa3`): early adoption buys a lead you hold while others catch up.

This is fully consistent with the governance doctrine: **publish the principle, steward the applied, keep the
tacit learning private.** Disclosure of the principle is not a leak — the moat was never the principle.

## 4. So — is the proposal worth making?

A balanced read (Tier 3; the government and competent advisors decide):

- **For:** it matches Canada's real strengths and its *own* current policy (CPFC spin‑off); it is specialty‑scale
  (hundreds of millions, not tens of billions); it is lower export‑sensitivity than front‑end logic; and Hˢ as an
  early‑adopted, auditable quality layer gives a *compounding, private* learning lead that disclosure cannot
  remove.
- **Against / caution:** a fab is capital‑intensive and slow; Hˢ is a *margin* improver, not a reason to build a
  fab; the value of the Hˢ layer is **unmeasured** until a pilot; and the durable advantage is the operational
  execution, which is hard and not guaranteed.
- **Honest synthesis:** **Yes, a specialty‑fab proposal can be worth making — but build the case on the fab's real
  moat (capital, talent, ecosystem, tacit learning), with Hˢ as the early‑mover *accelerant*, and publish the
  science without fear**, because ~96% of the advantage never becomes public. Do *not* build the case on Hˢ being
  secret or on a megafab Canada won't build.
