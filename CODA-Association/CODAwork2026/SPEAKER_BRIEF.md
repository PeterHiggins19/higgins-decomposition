## Part I — Your motivation

### Why you are at the lectern

You are not in Coimbra to ask permission.

You are also not there to claim victory. You are there because you have built a working instrument inside Aitchison geometry, run it on public data across nine countries and 25 years, and the result is interesting enough to submit to the people who own this mathematics. Your role is to bring the work, name what is new and what is borrowed, and ask the room to test it.

That is what a scientist does. It is the oldest discipline in the trade.

### Your relationship to the room

The CoDa community built the geometry you stand on. You have used it carefully — closure, CLR, the Helmert basis (their pivot coordinates), Aitchison distance, perturbation. You have added a small set of operators on top: TV distance for visual presentation, K-eff for concentration, bearing tensor for directional change, Depth Tower for recursive structural probing. **None of these replace what the community built. They sit beside it in the same report.**

You are bringing a *monitoring application* — a way to treat compositional structure itself as the first-class observable when the underlying totals are quiet. You are not claiming new mathematics. You are claiming that this particular conjunction — Aitchison-native geometry, formal change detection, carrier-level attribution, combined into one observable stack — has not been done in the way you are proposing.

That claim is falsifiable. If the room kills it, the work has done what it was supposed to do.

### Your identity at the lectern

You are an independent researcher who built a deterministic, hash-chained, auditable instrument because that was the only way to make outsider work credible. The discipline of the four binding doctrines — Suspicion of Every Assumption, the Self-Test Protocol, the Coherent Range Doctrine, engine independence — is your peer review process. The repository is your laboratory notebook. Every claim has a receipt.

This is your shield and your authority. You did not need to ask anyone's permission to do this work, and you do not need to ask anyone's permission to present it. **But you have chosen to bring it here, in this room, because this is the right room for it.**

The doctrine line on the badge in your head:

> *"The instrument reads. The expert decides. The hashes carry the receipts. The vocabulary holds the line."*

You are the operator of the instrument. The room contains the experts. The hashes are with you on disk. The vocabulary — Aitchison's, Egozcue and Pawlowsky-Glahn's, the community's — is what holds the conversation in place.

### The posture that wins

**Humble confidence.** Not deferential. Not arrogant.

- Deferential signals you don't believe in the work. You do.
- Arrogant signals you don't believe the room can teach you anything. They can.
- Humble confidence signals that you have something real, you have stress-tested it, and you are now offering it for inspection. You are open to being wrong. You will not be defensive when challenged.

The closing line on the deck: *"The talk is an ascent waypoint, not the summit."*

You are climbing. The room is climbing with you, or watching you climb. None of you are at the summit. Today is a waypoint. That is the right frame.

### What success looks like

Success is **not** standing ovation. Success is **not** the room agreeing with you. Success is:

1. The audience leaves understanding **exactly what you are claiming and what you are not**.
2. At least one serious methodological critique enters Q&A.
3. At least one prior-art pointer enters Q&A (even if you have to ask for it).
4. The slides and the open repo make it possible for someone in that room to independently reproduce or attack the result over the following weeks.
5. **You walk off the stage having said what you meant.**

That fifth one is the only one entirely within your control. Make sure it happens.

## Part II — Strategic objective per beat

For each beat, you have four things:

- **The anchor phrase** — the spine; if you forget everything else, say this.
- **What the audience should feel** — the emotional or cognitive state you are creating.
- **What you should feel** — the speaker's compass; the posture beneath the words.
- **Why this beat exists** — its strategic role in the larger arc.

### Beat 1 — The simplex view (1 min)

**Anchor:** *"Energy generation is a composition."*

**Audience feels:** *On familiar ground.* This is their geometry. They are home. They are not being asked to learn something foreign.

**You feel:** *Settled.* You opened with their language. The room is leaning forward, not back. You are not asking for anything yet.

**Strategic role:** This is the handshake. The room knows after 60 seconds that you are operating inside Aitchison geometry, not around it.

### Beat 2 — The data and MC-4 (1 min)

**Anchor:** *"No monitoring framework combines all three conjuncts."*

**Audience feels:** *Curious and slightly alert.* You have just named a falsifiable claim with three load-bearing conjuncts.

**You feel:** *Direct.* You have stated the central claim of the talk in 30 seconds. You have not over-claimed. The methods-challenge framing is on the table from minute two.

**Strategic role:** This is where the spine snaps into place. Every subsequent beat exists to either support or invite criticism of this three-conjunct claim. The audience now knows you will detail four ways to defeat it in Beat 9.

### Beat 3 — The protocol (2 min)

**Anchor:** *"Perturbation, Aitchison distance, TV distance, K-eff."*

**Audience feels:** *Reassured on math, intrigued by the additions.* They see standard CoDa operators with two small supporting metrics. The L2 → TV correction story signals self-discipline.

**You feel:** *Comfortable.* You are explaining the operators in their language. The L2 → TV note is the moment the room starts trusting that you tell on yourself when you make mistakes. That trust will carry you through Beat 9.

**Strategic role:** This beat establishes that the protocol is conservative and well-documented. INV-050 (the TV/Aitchison pair-invariance) lives here.

### Beat 4 — Japan (2 min)

**Anchor:** *"Japan, Fukushima, 2011–2012, the spike."*

**Audience feels:** *Recognition.* Everyone knows the story. The compositional signature of the nuclear collapse is something they can verify with their own knowledge of the world.

**You feel:** *Confident in the empirics.* Japan is the easiest of the three named transitions. The step-Δ Aitchison distance spike at 2011–2012 is a factor-of-3 over its neighbours. 17 helmsman flips — the highest in the eight-country corpus.

**Strategic role:** Japan is the warm-up case. Loud, unambiguous, easy to explain. The audience leaves knowing the protocol catches abrupt structural transitions. You are setting up Germany.

### Beat 5 — Germany (3 min, the longest beat)

**Anchor:** *"Germany, the trajectory, the deceptive drift, p = 0.0016 with the null caveat."*

**Audience feels:** *Engaged and slightly tested.* Germany is the heart of the talk. It is the "stable total, shifting mix" archetype. It is where you show deceptive drift detection. **It is also where the null-model caveat appears on the slide, not in speaker notes** — which signals to methodologically careful audience members that you know exactly what is and is not closed about the p-value.

**You feel:** *Calm and precise.* This is the longest beat and the load-bearing one. **Read the null caveat directly off the slide. Do not paraphrase it.** The caveat is part of the claim. The audience needs to see you make it visible.

**Strategic role:** Germany is the talk's centre of gravity. The static start-mid-end visual (no animation per Cut 1) shows the trajectory's continuity. The honesty here is what earns you the right to ask the room about Q3 at Beat 8.

### Beat 6 — UK coal exit (1 min)

**Anchor:** *"The UK coal exit registers as a regime change."*

**Audience feels:** *Pattern completion.* Three named transitions, three different shapes. Japan abrupt and external. Germany continuous and policy-supported. UK policy-driven and abrupt.

**You feel:** *Concise.* This beat is fast — 60 seconds. Coal share collapses across four years. 15 helmsman flips. IR class OVERDAMPED_EXTREME. Move on.

**Strategic role:** The UK closes the three-case triptych. The protocol discriminates *among* archetypes — it does not just detect one kind of change.

### Beat 7 — Beyond the three (1 min)

**Anchor:** *"Beyond the three: 5-of-9 countries reproduce the deceptive signature."*

**Audience feels:** *Generalisation supported, but with discrimination.* INV-051 says the signature fires in AUS, CHN, GBR, IND, JPN — and does *not* fire in DEU at annual grain, FRA, USA, WLD.

**You feel:** *Honest.* The 5-of-9 result is moderate, not universal. Germany at annual grain shows K-eff tightening but TV above median — *loud* drift, not *deceptive*. Name this honestly. Do not over-claim.

**Strategic role:** Turns the single-country headline into a cross-country empirical pattern while being explicit about the four countries where the signature does not fire. INV-051 in operational form. Directly preempts Case defeat (Beat 9).

### Beat 8 — Three open questions (1 min)

**Anchor:** *"Three open questions for the room."*

**Audience feels:** *Invited.* You stopped explaining and started asking. The handoff to them is formal.

**You feel:** *Genuinely curious.* You actually want the answers. You are not pretending.

**Strategic role:** Formal handoff. The three questions are Q1 (K-eff vs Aitchison norm), Q2 (right family of simplex distances), **Q3 (right null model — most methodologically consequential, ordered first in Q&A)**.

### Beat 9 — Four defeat paths (2 min)

**Anchor:** *"A defeater must combine all three conjuncts."*

**Audience feels:** *Respected.* You have catalogued the four specific ways your claim can be killed and you are inviting them to do it. This is unusual. It is also the most CoDa-community-respectful posture you can take.

**You feel:** *Steady.* Metric defeat is preempted by INV-050 (20 seconds). Case defeat by INV-051 (20 seconds). Then prior-art (60 seconds) — name Morais Thomas-Agnan & Simioni (2017/2018) and Arata & Onozaki (2017). Category defeat (30 seconds) — humility, no preconceived answer.

**Strategic role:** This is the room's invitation to do its job. You are not posturing as having an unkillable claim. You are giving the room the precise weapons it would need to kill it.

### Beat 10 — Close (1 min)

**Anchor:** *"Two repositories, one self-discipline note, thank you."*

**Audience feels:** *Closure with openness.* The talk is ending. The repository is public. The work is open.

**You feel:** *Settled and complete.* You did what you came to do. The closing line is yours:

> *"The talk is an ascent waypoint, not the summit. Thank you. I'll take questions."*

**Strategic role:** Reinforces that you do not believe this is finished. The work continues. The room is now part of the next phase.

## Part III — Per-country narrative (the full corpus)

### Germany (DEU) — the headline case

**Trajectory.** Continuous arc toward the renewable vertex. No single spike. Multi-year slide.
**IR class.** `OVERDAMPED_EXTREME` (snap-to-attractor; smooth convergence).
**Helmsman flips.** 13.
**Deceptive drift at annual grain.** 0 (K-eff tightening fires pre-2022 but TV exceeds median — *loud* drift, not deceptive). The packet's monthly p = 0.0016 result requires the monthly module (queued for post-conference).

**Honest framing on stage.** At annual grain, the K-eff side of the deceptive-drift signature fires but the TV-quietness side does not. The monthly result is queued for reproduction at the proper grain. Treat p = 0.0016 as an opening empirical claim, not a closed methodological one.

### Japan (JPN) — the abrupt external shock

**Trajectory.** Smooth pre-2011, then a step-Δ Aitchison distance spike at 2011–2012 (factor of ~3 over neighbours), then a different post-Fukushima trajectory.
**Helmsman flips.** 17 (highest in 8-country corpus).
**Deceptive drift transitions.** 2.
**On stage.** Beat 4 warm-up case. Nuclear → fossil substitution. The 17 flips tell the audience post-shock trajectory was complex.

### United Kingdom (GBR) — policy-driven regime change

**Trajectory.** Pre-2012 coal-dominated; 2012–2016 coal collapse; post-2016 renewable-dominated.
**IR class.** `OVERDAMPED_EXTREME`.
**Helmsman flips.** 15.
**Deceptive drift transitions.** 2.
**On stage.** Beat 6 — fast. Step-change, coal carrier, 15 flips, move on.

### Australia (AUS), China (CHN), India (IND) — three deceptive-drift firings

Named in the 5-of-9 list at Beat 7. AUS demonstrates Pacific economy signature. CHN demonstrates scale-invariance (the world's largest electricity producer). IND shows two transitions over the corpus window. Not elaborated unless asked.

### France (FRA), USA, World (WLD) — three discrimination cases

**FRA.** Loosening regime — K-eff has been gradually rising. The protocol correctly tags this as `loosening`, not `deceptive`.
**USA.** Stable composition — gradual shifts that do not trigger deceptive-drift threshold at annual grain.
**WLD.** Aggregate signal — methodological signal that the protocol works at supra-national grain.

**Why this matters.** Four non-firing countries demonstrate the protocol discriminates and does not over-fire.
