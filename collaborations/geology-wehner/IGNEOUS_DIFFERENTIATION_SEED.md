# Igneous differentiation as compositional dynamics — the seed study

**Peter's supposition:** a cooling rock, from a liquid (melt) state, crystallizes according to **agglomeration** and the **phase points of the chemistry of the composition** — a dynamical account of how crystals develop in a chemical blend as a function of **phase temperatures and cooling speed.** In geological terms: **fractional crystallization / igneous differentiation modelled as a compositional dynamical system on the simplex** — the melt composition follows a trajectory as it cools through successive phase points (liquidus / cotectic / peritectic), minerals agglomerating and crystallizing out and reshaping the residual-melt composition.

## The gap = the opportunity
The realized work is **cross-sectional** (snapshots of rock compositions). A built **dynamical cooling simulation** matching the exact supposition — melt trajectory resolved through phase points vs cooling rate — was **not** found; it reads as a conceptual seed not yet realized. That gap is the natural **co-authorable** study: turn the cross-sectional CoDa geochemistry into a *dynamical* model of differentiation.

## Sketch of the study
1. Treat the **residual melt** as a composition evolving on the simplex as temperature falls.
2. Crystallization = mass leaving the melt into agglomerating mineral phases at phase points (liquidus/cotectic), set by the chemistry and the cooling rate.
3. Read the trajectory with CNT (bearing = differentiation direction; helmsman = the oxide driving each step; regime tripwires = phase-point crossings; deceptive drift = a minor oxide doing outsized structural work near a cotectic).
4. **Benchmark against established petrology** — e.g. **MELTS / rhyolite-MELTS** thermodynamic models and known liquid-lines-of-descent — so the dynamical reading is validated, not asserted.
