The faceted read

Finite atlases and the curvature of the high-dimensional compositional manifold
STATUS: concept & direction · geometry solid · value on real data is the open upside
The phrase. A high-dimensional composition traces a curved manifold. An exact D=4 CNQ chart is a flat facet laid against it. A finite atlas of overlapping charts is therefore a faceted read of that curved object — a polygon standing in for a circle. Add more overlapping facets and the polygon tightens toward the true curve. The redundancy you build in is exactly what buys back the curvature.

1 · The picture

Think of every sample as a point on a smooth, curved surface living in many dimensions (one per part, minus closure). The motion down-core is a path on that surface. You cannot see the whole curved object at once — but each exact four-part chart is a flat tangent panel that touches it perfectly along its own little patch. Cover the surface with enough overlapping panels and you have a model of the whole thing; but between any two panels, wherever you assumed a straight edge, the real surface was curving. That gap is the unmodelled curvature.

2 · The geometric law behind it

Each side-view / tangent direction hands you a single number — how far the surface reaches that way (its support function). Curvature is essentially how that number bends as you sweep the direction. So:

one direction → one number (a facet, a flat edge) finite directions → a polygon: flat between samples all directions → the exact curve (curvature everywhere)

An infinite set of tangential projections is required to reveal every nuance of the surface — finitely many always leave a faceted approximation, with the real curve hidden in the gaps you didn't sample. This is one fact wearing three hats: the missing wedge in tomography, the bump you can hide between silhouettes, and the flat edge between chart panels. In every case, only redundant, well-spread sampling closes the gap.

3 · From limitation to dial

This reframes "CNQ is exact only at D=4" from a ceiling into a resolution control. You are not stuck at one lossy high-D projection; you choose how finely to facet the manifold by how many overlapping charts you lay down and where. And the faceting can be adaptive: concentrate facets — add overlap — exactly where curvature (higher-order coupling) is anticipated, and stay coarse where the surface is nearly flat. The diversity-versus-overlap trade-off becomes a design knob, not a constraint.

Why it's exact where it counts. Each facet is a true tangent (D=4 CNQ is native, not approximate), and the gluing on shared parts is lossless for the compositional move confirmed. So refinement only ever adds resolution — it never trades away the exactness already in hand.

4 · Why this is a use direction — the feedback / feedforward amplifier

The faceted read isn't just a way to see; it's a loop that gets sharper with use, and an expert in the loop is the amplifier.

Feedback (data & expert → method)
Real core-scan data plus a geologist's judgement say which facets matter, where the surface is actually curving, and which reconstructions ring true against independent evidence. That tells the atlas where to densify and how to anchor — the method tunes itself to the rock.
Feedforward (refined model → next look)
A better-faceted model then predicts where to look — which depths, which sub-systems, which couplings deserve a finer chart — guiding sampling and interpretation before the next pass.

An expert closing that loop compounds it: the instrument proposes facets, the expert judges and re-anchors, the judgement reshapes the atlas, which proposes sharper facets. Deterministic instrument + domain expert = returns that build on each other — exactly the posture Hs is built for. The honest part: how much an expert with real data draws out of this is genuinely open — plausibly more than can be guessed in advance, which is the reason to run it, not a claim that it will. to test
