# Q&A bench card — Category defeat

**When to use:** if asked whether this is really a new monitoring category, or whether it's just an application of existing CoDa.

---

## The 30-second answer

> *"Honestly — we don't know. This may be a new monitoring category: composition monitoring as a primary observable. Or it may be at most a useful application note inside existing CoDa. We have no preconceived answer. We welcome the room's view, including the view that the MC-4 label is too strong. The work itself stands either way — the energy case is methodologically sound, the engine is deterministic and hash-chained, the findings are catalogued and falsifiable. Whether it deserves a new label is genuinely the room's call."*

## If pressed

**"Why not just call it an application?"**
> *"That's a defensible position. If the room's consensus is that this is application-note status, we will narrow the framing and publish accordingly — likely as a Math. Geosci. application note rather than a methodological paper. That's a perfectly acceptable outcome."*

**"What would make it a new category in your view?"**
> *"A new monitoring category, in the way we mean it, would have: a distinct observable stack (Aitchison + change-detection + carrier-attribution as a combined unit), a distinct claim language (the three-conjunct MC-4 framing), and distinct operational tooling (the engine + the four doctrines + the catalog discipline). Whether those distinctions earn the 'new category' label is the room's judgment, not ours."*

**"Wouldn't 'compositional monitoring' just be CoDa applied to monitoring?"**
> *"Yes — that's exactly the application-note version. We're not contesting that framing if it's what the community thinks fits. The talk's commitment is that the work is open and falsifiable either way."*

## What to NOT claim

- Do not say *"this is definitely a new monitoring category"* — that's the overclaim the talk explicitly avoids.
- Do not get defensive. Category defeat is the most philosophical of the four; it's not a falsifiability failure, it's a labelling decision.
- Do not collapse the work. Whether the label is "new category" or "application note", the engine and findings remain unchanged.

## What to ALLOW

- The room may rule "application note." That's a fine outcome. The talk is structured to survive it.
- The room may rule "new category." That's also fine, and reinforces the publication track.
- The room may say "ask us again in a year after community use." Also fine, and a graceful invitation to keep working.
