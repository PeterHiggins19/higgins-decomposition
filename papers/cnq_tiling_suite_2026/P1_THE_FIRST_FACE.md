## The hook (start here, first‑time reader)

A loudspeaker radiating into a room is solving, without being told, a problem in *compositional* geometry: a
conserved budget of correction, apportioned across the cabinet's dimensions, the shares always summing to the
whole. Twenty‑five years of building those instruments produced a single surprising fact, and P1 is that fact:

> **At four parts, a composition _is_ a quaternion** — the same mathematical object that describes a rotation in
> three‑dimensional space — and a change in the mixture is read as an *exact rotation,* to the last decimal a
> computer can hold (residual ≈ 4.4×10⁻¹⁶). Larger mixtures are tiled into exact four‑part charts and rebuilt to
> the floating‑point floor (≈ 4.1×10⁻¹² at a million parts — *measured reconstruction, not "lossless"*).

That is the bedrock: before anything is interpreted, it is read **exactly.** But the reason this matters to a
working scientist, an engineer, or a regulator is what the exact reading lets you *see that you could not see
before.*

## The first face — the basic blindness P1 reveals

Every ordinary monitor watches **totals and levels**: a threshold, an alarm, an average. P1's exact relational
reading exposes what that kind of monitor is **blind** to:

> **Ratio‑blind.** A scalar / total / threshold monitor sees levels and alarms — and is *confidently silent*
> about the **ratios.** A mixture can tighten toward a failure mode — its *proportions* drifting — while every
> raw level still looks calm and in‑spec. The total says "fine." The composition has already turned.

This is not a flaw to apologize for; it is a **named, recoverable class of event** — *sub‑threshold
concentration drift* (the "silent" or "deceptive" drift) — and P1's exact log‑ratio reading is the instrument
that makes it visible. The same fact, stated as a
gift rather than a warning: *the meaning is in the proportions, and the proportions are exactly readable.*
