## 1 · Introduction

Compositional data carry their information in ratios, and the isometric log‑ratio (ILR) transformation gives that information an orthonormal Euclidean coordinate system in which Aitchison distances, inner products, and isometries behave exactly as their real‑space counterparts. This paper does **not** propose a new geometry for compositional data. It takes the Aitchison/ILR geometry as given and observes that, at four parts, that geometry admits an exact algebraic realization in the unit quaternions — and that this local exactness can be carried to arbitrarily high dimension through a connected atlas of overlapping four‑part charts.

The observation did not begin at a whiteboard. It was forced by a physical system. A loudspeaker radiating into a room has, at low frequency, an isotropic 4π pattern; as wavelength approaches the cabinet's dimensions that balance breaks, and the total correction is a **fixed budget** (6.02 dB = 20·log₁₀2) apportioned across the box's dimensions and summing, by construction, to a constant. That is a three‑part composition with a conserved total — closure forced by physics, not chosen by a modeler — and the engineering quantity one designs for is the *coherence* of the summed field at the listening surface. The compositional structure, and the centrality of coherence, were therefore present in the instrument before they were recognized as compositional‑data structure. We take this origin seriously because it is the reason the construction below is exact rather than approximate: there were no researcher degrees of freedom in the place where models usually go wrong.

**The D=4 identification.** For a four‑part composition, the ILR image is a point in ℝ³. Equipped with the natural cross‑product structure, ℝ³ is the Lie algebra 𝔰𝔲(2) of pure imaginary quaternions, so the ILR coordinate can be read as a pure quaternion **v**. A unit quaternion q ∈ S³ ≅ SU(2) then acts by the sandwich product **v′ = q v q\***, an exact, norm‑preserving rotation that reproduces the corresponding Aitchison isometry. Because the operation is bound to the algebraic constraints of SU(2) rather than realized through trigonometric evaluation, it holds to the floating‑point floor: on real data the residual against the direct Aitchison computation sits at machine epsilon (≈ 4.4×10⁻¹⁶). This is best read as the *geometric completion* of the orthonormal‑coordinate program — the point at which the ILR coordinate stops being merely a vector and becomes an element of a group action that the four‑part case happens to realize exactly.

**The high‑dimensional mechanism.** A composition of dimension D > 4 is tiled into overlapping four‑part charts that share pivot components. Each chart carries the exact D=4 algebra above; the explicit, deterministic transition maps between charts are coordinate changes in ILR space; and the full centered‑log‑ratio state is reconstructed by solving the connected system the charts define. Reconstruction is exact **iff** the chart co‑occurrence graph is connected. The practical cost is conditioning: a linear chain of charts has graph diameter O(D), so floating‑point error accumulates as O(D·ε) and degrades at large D. Structuring the atlas as a **balanced tree** removes cycles (no phase‑loop conflict) and compresses the diameter to O(log D) — roughly ten transitions at D = 10⁶ — bounding the accumulated drift to ≈ 10·ε. The measured reconstruction at D = 10⁶ is ≈ 4.1×10⁻¹² (floating‑point accumulation, **not** bit‑exact mathematical identity — a distinction we keep explicit throughout).

**What is and is not claimed.** The per‑chart exactness at D=4 is exact to the IEEE floor; the high‑dimensional reconstruction is a *floating‑point* reconstruction whose residual is bounded by the atlas diameter, not a claim of mathematical identity at arbitrary D. The transformations themselves are not new — they are the Aitchison/ILR operations the field already uses. What is new is the exact SU(2) realization at four parts and the connected‑atlas mechanism that carries that exactness, deterministically and reproducibly, to high dimension. The construction is deterministic and hash‑receipted: identical inputs yield identical outputs and a matching content hash across platforms, with an open reference implementation, language‑agnostic pseudocode, and a replication kit provided.

**Why this may be useful.** Domains where compositions run to thousands or millions of parts — microbiome, geochemistry, single‑cell — face a tension between exactness and scale that truncated or statistical reductions resolve by sacrificing one. A native‑four‑part atlas offers a different trade: local exactness everywhere, global reconstruction at a cost that grows only with the logarithm of the dimension, every step auditable to a receipt. Whether that trade is the right one for a given problem is a question for the domain; the contribution here is the construction and the honest accounting of what it costs.
