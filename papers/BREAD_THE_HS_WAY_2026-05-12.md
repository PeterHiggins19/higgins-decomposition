## The narrative (verbal, memorizable, ~1900 words)

The loaf is a composition. Before it is a loaf it is four things: flour, water, salt, and yeast. Sometimes a fifth — a little sugar, a little fat — but you don't need them. Those four make bread. And every loaf you have ever eaten was a different ratio of those four. That is the whole framework. The loaf sits on a simplex. Its components sum to one. You cannot add more flour without taking something else away. You cannot make 1.1 of a loaf. Whatever you put in, the proportions add up to the whole.

That is the first thing the framework knows about bread. It knows that the loaf is a *proportional* system. And because it is proportional, it is governed by simplex mathematics — the same mathematics that governs the energy mix in a country's electricity grid, the composition of a wetland's species, the share of sectors in a national economy. The bread does not know any of this. The bread is just bread. But the bread *obeys* the same constraints, and that means the same instrument that reads electricity grids can read bread.

The four ingredients are the carriers. In the language of the framework, the simplex has four dimensions when the bread is plain. Five when it has sugar. Six when it has fat. The dough's K-effective — the number of carriers meaningfully participating in the structure — is the first dimensionless number the baker reads. If you forgot the salt, K-eff drops to three and the loaf reads as flatter than it should. If you doubled the yeast, K-eff is still four, but the dynamics will be wrong. K-eff doesn't tell you everything. It tells you how alive the structure is.

Bakers don't talk about K-eff. Bakers talk about hydration. Hydration is the water-to-flour ratio, expressed as a percentage — 65%, 75%, 85%. These percentages are pure simplex thinking. A 75% hydration loaf has the same dynamics whether you scaled it for one loaf or a thousand. The proportion is what governs the rise, the crumb, the crust. Magnitude is irrelevant. Bakers already think in Aitchison geometry. They just don't know they do.

You start with the flour and the water. Just those two for a few minutes — the autolyse. The flour absorbs the water. The two carriers start talking to each other through the gluten proteins. No yeast yet. No salt yet. You are forming the substrate on which the next two carriers will operate. This is the initial composition — the unmixed simplex. The dough has structure but no dynamics. The instrument would read K-eff equal to two and TV — the total variation, the structural velocity — equal to zero. Static dough. Living potential.

Then you add the yeast. And then the salt. Now the structure has all four carriers. Now the dynamics begin. The yeast eats the sugars in the flour and breathes out carbon dioxide. The salt slows the yeast down and tightens the gluten. The water carries everything around. The flour holds it all in suspension. Each carrier has a job. Each carrier's proportion determines how the others will behave. This is the load-bearing claim of the whole framework: in a compositional system, the carriers are not independent. The simplex forces them to be in relationship. Change one, and the others must respond.

You knead. Or you fold. Or both. Kneading is the operation that develops the gluten — that converts the random protein chains in the flour into long elastic structures that can hold the gas the yeast will produce. In framework language, kneading is the operation that preserves the simplex while developing internal structure. The proportions don't change. The composition stays the same. But the *organization* of the composition gets sharper. The Aitchison metric reads the same dough before and after kneading because Aitchison geometry is scale-invariant in the magnitude direction. But the internal structure is different. The instrument can read this difference. This is what the CNT engine does — Composition Navigation by Tensor — it reads the structural organization of a proportional system. The dough before kneading and the dough after kneading have the same composition but different navigation.

Now the first rise. You leave the dough alone. The yeast does its work. The dough doubles. Time becomes a carrier. The framework reads not just the proportions but their evolution. Total Variation — TV — measures how fast the structure is moving through time. In a sleeping fridge-retarded dough, TV is low. In a warm-kitchen quick-rise dough, TV is high. Same composition. Different velocity. And here is where the framework starts to predict — TV that rises quickly and then goes calm is the signature of deceptive drift. The dough that suddenly stops moving in a warm kitchen is not done rising; it is dying. The structure is concentrating around fewer carriers because the yeast is exhausting itself. The baker who knows TV reads the slowing as warning, not as readiness.

Then you fold. The folding step is the Helmsman — the baker's hand reading the dough and steering it. You feel the dough's surface, its tension, its temperature, its smell. You decide to fold or not to fold. You are the operator. The instrument reads. The expert decides. This is the open-loop doctrine in the kitchen. The framework does not tell the baker when to fold. The framework reads what the dough is doing. The baker decides whether to act. A loaf where the timer decides everything is not a baker's loaf. It is a machine's loaf.

The second rise — the proof. Now the structure is mostly formed. The shape is set. The yeast does the last work before the heat arrives. And here EITT becomes visible at the dinner-table scale. A bread proofed for twelve hours at cool temperature reads the same as a bread proofed for one hour at warm temperature — when you measure the compositional entropy of the dough as it ferments. The geometric mean of the proofing windows is invariant across the temporal scale. This is what EITT says in the wild. The slow loaf and the fast loaf, if their compositional dynamics survive the same geometric-mean compression, will produce the same flavor structure in the mouth. Different absolute times. Same structural composition. Time is collapsed; flavor is preserved. A baker who has done both knows this in their hands. The framework knows it as a published theorem.

Then comes the baking. The dough enters the oven as a compositional structure and exits as a transformed structure. Baking is the one-way operation in the framework. M² = I in the simplex; you can rotate the dough through the metric and rotate back. But baking is not a metric rotation. Baking is an irreversible thermodynamic event. You cannot un-bake. The Maillard reaction, the gelatinization of starches, the protein coagulation — these are entropy-increasing one-way doors. The framework reads dough before and after. The framework does not pretend the door swings both ways. This is honest treatment of uncertainty. You measure what you have. You do not claim what you cannot reverse.

The crust and the crumb. The finished loaf has two carriers of *structure* — the crust on the outside, the crumb on the inside. They are coherent with each other: the same dough produces them both, the same heat shapes them both, and yet they are different. This is the CHSH joint-coherence diagnostic — INV-035 — in bread form. The crust and the crumb are joint-coherent within the loaf, bounded by a structural Tsirelson-like limit. You cannot have an arbitrarily airy crumb and an arbitrarily crisp crust together; the dough's algebra refuses it. The twin-quaternion factoring at D=8 — INV-029 — is the deeper mathematical statement of why. The dough's structural algebra factors deterministically into crust-pair and crumb-pair at a specific dimensional level. Bakers know this empirically. The framework knows it algebraically.

Now the kill conditions — the ways the bread fails. KILL-001 in bread: too much yeast and the structure collapses in the oven because the gluten exhausts before the heat sets it. Too little hydration and the loaf reads as dead even before baking — K-eff is too low. Dead starter and TV stays at zero through the entire proof — nothing is moving. Salt forgotten — the yeast accelerates uncontrollably, K-eff briefly rises and then crashes. Carrier artificial — substituting almond flour for wheat flour and expecting the gluten dynamics is the worst failure mode, because the loaf looks legitimate, the math runs, the instrument reads, but the structure is sand. KILL-3.3 in bread form: a bake that looks fine and falls apart.

The yeast is the component of power. This is where the framing matters most. The yeast is the active carrier, the one that does *work* on the others. The salt structures, the water carries, the flour holds — but the yeast moves the simplex. It changes the proportions over time. It is the only carrier that grows. The yeast is the dimensionless engine of the system. In framework terms, the yeast is the Yeast Factor — INV-060 STAGED — the active power-carrying carrier whose presence is required for the dynamics to exist at all. The framework recognizes that not every carrier in a proportional system is symmetric. Some carriers are passive — structure, vehicle. Some carriers are active — engine, power. The yeast is the framework's archetypal active carrier. Without it, the loaf is a paste. With it, the loaf is bread. And the dimensionless Q-factor of the yeast — how vigorously it ferments per unit mass, how quickly it doubles, how long it survives in the cold — is the loaf's Thiele-Small parameter. The yeast's Q determines the loaf's alignment.

The baker is the operator. The baker holds Breaker 16. The baker decides when the bread is done. No alarm tells the baker the bread is done; the bread tells the baker, through the tap on the crust, the lift of the loaf, the color of the crumb when you cut it open. The framework gives the baker readings. The baker integrates the readings with thirty years of standing next to ovens and decides. HUF-GOV protects judgment. HUF-CLS optimizes correction. A bread-machine is HUF-CLS — closed-loop, automated, you put the ingredients in and a loaf comes out. A baker's hand-shaped sourdough is HUF-GOV — open-loop, every decision held by the operator. The loaf is what survives the operator's judgment.

So that is bread the Hs way. The carriers are four. The simplex is the dough. The kneading preserves the composition while developing internal structure. The rising introduces dynamics in time. K-eff reads how alive the structure is. TV reads how fast it is moving. The folding is Helmsman. The proofing exhibits EITT — the geometric-mean entropy is invariant across temporal compression; slow loaf reads the same as fast loaf in flavor structure. The baking is the irreversible transformation, the one-way door. The crust and crumb are joint-coherent within a Tsirelson-like bound. The kill conditions name the nineteen ways bread fails. The yeast is the active carrier — the component of power, the dimensionless engine, the Thiele-Small Q of the loaf. The baker is the operator — the breakpoint, the open loop, the last breaker.

The instrument reads. The expert decides. The hashes carry the receipts. The vocabulary holds the line. And the dough rises while the baker watches.

INV-060's STAGED status pre-existed this narrative; the bread metaphor confirms that the framework had already recognized "active carrier" as a structural category.
